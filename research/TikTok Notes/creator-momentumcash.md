## 1. Simulated playtesters

His main format. Have Claude role-play a specific kid, find where they get bored, confused or stuck, then fix it and repeat. All of these are in the `roblox-player-sims` skill.

- Impatient kid: improve the first 60 seconds until they stay. (50K)
- 7-year-old who can't follow complicated instructions: improve the tutorial until they play without help.
- Impatient 8-year-old: find the first 3 moments they get bored.
- 8-year-old with rewards stripped out: is the core loop fun on its own? (146K)
- Kid who has played every day for a month: what still excites them, what got repetitive? (31K)
- Group of kids arguing about which game to play: what are their objections to yours? (22K)
- Two best friends: which moments do they talk about at school the next day? (38K)
- Kid showing the game to a best friend: what do they show off, what do they skip? (12K)
- 14-year-old showing it to an 8-year-old brother: what does the younger kid need help with?
- Kid with a friend watching over their shoulder: which moments make the friend want to join?
- Kid trying to make friends laugh: add more chances for funny moments.
- Kid playing with sound off: which events do they miss? Fix visual feedback.
- Kid half-watching YouTube on another screen: which moments need too much attention?
- 8-year-old joining a server full of better players: what's intimidating?
- 7-year-old exploring the map for the first time: what do they overlook? Fix visual cues.
- 7-year-old browsing the shop: what's boring or confusing?
- Kid trying to buy an upgrade: why does it look pointless?
- 7-year-old choosing between your thumbnail and 20 others: why would they scroll past?
- Teen who only plays with friends: what would make it more fun in a group?
- YouTuber recording your game: improve until there are 3 clip-worthy moments.
- Kid with a strict screen-time limit: improve until he'd beg for more time.
- Easily distracted kid: always give them a reason to keep playing.
- Kid with a $10/week allowance: improve until he wants to spend it in your game.
- 8-year-old with a parent's credit card: improve until he buys something.

> The last two are a red flag. Designing to get kids to spend a parent's card is the kind of thing that gets games reported and runs into Roblox's rules for under-13s. The skill rewrites these as "does the purchase feel worth it and clear?", not "make them spend".

## 2. Study something outside Roblox, apply it to your game

- Nintendo: animation timing, sound, feedback on simple actions. (92K)
- Duolingo: making small accomplishments feel rewarding → progression feedback.
- Pokémon: collection psychology, for any collect-them game.
- Five Nights at Freddy's: tension from limited information.
- Elden Ring: environmental clues instead of instructions → exploration.
- Animal Crossing: emotional attachment through customisation.
- Rockstar: small environmental details that make a world feel alive (find 5 to add).
- Minecraft: curiosity about what's around the corner.
- Rocket League: simple controls, lots of skill expression.
- Hitchcock's bomb theory: suspense from knowing danger is coming.
- Reality TV: multiplayer mechanics that create drama naturally.
- Museums: interactive exhibits to fix boring map areas.
- Supermarket layouts: encourage exploration of the map.
- Fast-food menus: visual hierarchy and simple choices → redesign your shop. (26K)
- Mobile game stores: make every product's value obvious. (29K)
- Valorant / Counter-Strike: what makes shooters fun, then build with ForgeGUI MCP and agent playtesting.
- Blox Fruits / Steal a Brainrot / Dress to Impress: how they make Robux, then have 3 agents debate the best monetization for your game. (17K)

## 3. Game ideas

- Dead game method: shut-down multiplayer games with passionate communities. → [[dead-game-method]]
- Trend hijacking: scan trending TikToks, indie Steam games and memes for ideas with no popular Roblox version yet. Scope to a 48-hour build. Rank by search demand, competition, ease. (7K)
- Indie games gaining traction on Twitter (#indiedev) that could work on Roblox.
- Games from 2005–2015 with mechanics modern Roblox hasn't explored.
- Board games → real-time multiplayer.
- Real jobs (baggage handler, air traffic controller, demolition, wildlife rescue) → job sims. Also: 15 unusual jobs, pick the most stressful/funny/satisfying task from each, top 5 as multiplayer concepts. (11K)
- Ridiculous real-world competitions; unusual psychology experiments on cooperation/competition.
- Analyse 100 games in your genre, find a gap nobody serves well.
- Original > copies: AI finally makes ambitious original mechanics buildable, yet most people use it to clone faster. (9K)
- His 5 "millionaire" ideas: build-a-zoo (catch animals, charge admission), human claw machine, bunker wars, don't wake the giant, fake your death.

## 4. Retention and design checklists

**5 retention rules (53K):**
1. Give a goal in the first 10 seconds.
2. Show what's coming next (future rewards, upgrades, milestones).
3. Small wins early, not after 20 minutes.
4. Reveal content gradually.
5. Leave "unfinished business" so they have a reason to return.

**5 steps to a better game (13K):** borrow an addictive mechanic from outside Roblox; pass the 10-second test; design for friend groups; simulate your audience; track drop-offs with Funnel Analytics.

**Other one-liners:**
- 10-second test: a new player knows the goal without reading anything. (10K)
- Every action needs feedback (purchases, upgrades, tasks). → [[prompt-visual-feedback]]
- Find the 10 heaviest models and optimise them without changing how they look. (39K)
- Low-end phone checklist: FPS, memory, effects, UI during real gameplay. (7K)
- Test UI at different phone screen sizes for overlap and hidden buttons. (13K)
- Mobile audit of buttons, menus, controls.
- Find empty, repetitive map areas and chances for new mechanics.
- Dynamic weather that transitions smoothly and affects gameplay (slippery rain, fog, lightning). (4K)

## 5. Art, models, animation, sound

- **Blueprint before modelling (22K):** give Claude 3 reference images (hero shot, exploded view, 360 turnaround). Have it write down exact dimensions, part counts, colours, materials and moving parts, then build from the blueprint, not by guessing from images.
- **Animation critic loop (38K):** Claude animates in Blender, a separate critic agent reviews frame by frame (stiff moves, awkward poses, timing, transitions), Claude fixes keyframes and curves, repeat until it passes, then import.
- **Model critic loop (23K):** find models with bad proportions, low-quality textures or off-style looks; generate better references with ChatGPT; a critic agent compares each model to its reference until it passes.
- **Sound audit (9K):** find sounds that are too loud, annoying or off-style; make replacements with ElevenLabs; balance volume, pitch and EQ.
- He says he turned his whole 3D modelling workflow into a Claude skill (Blender → into your game). It's free in his Discord, not public.
- Free 3D UI pack in his Discord. 3D interactive menus are where he thinks Roblox UI is heading (from a dev building "Pizza Hour").
- **Skip:** his "steal premium UIs" video (screenshot paid UI packs on BuiltByBit and have Claude copy them). That's copying someone's paid work, and the vault's own rules say no copied UI.

## 6. Marketing, ads, thumbnails

- Hidden-name strategy (22K): viral videos where the game name is hidden, so comments fill with "what game is this?". Use one recognisable character, test hooks, double down on what gets comments.
- 3 ad thumbnails that are truly different concepts (gameplay / reward / crazy moment), small budgets, scale the one with the lowest **cost per play**. (14K) Pairs with [[roblox-ads-and-thumbnails]].
- Top 20 games in your genre → why their thumbnails convert → 3 originals.
- 30 social video concepts for your game based on why viral Roblox videos get shared.
- Optimise title, description and tags for search.
- Content advice: post a lot (he says 10/day); attention is leverage, and a small engaged niche beats a big uninterested one.

## 7. Analytics and routines (Claude on a schedule)

- Daily 8 AM report: yesterday's players, earnings, playtime; save to desktop with the 3 biggest things to fix.
- Daily ad check: clicks, spend, which ads bring players; note which to pause, keep, redesign.
- **Weekly mistakes → rules (4K):** have Claude read its logs from the week, find every broken script or correction you had to make, turn each into a one-line rule in CLAUDE.md. Every run starts smarter.
- Model the numbers: give it acquisition, retention and monetization data and model what has to change to reach $10K/month.

## 8. Agent workflow

- Three agents: one researches, one builds, one reviews; they catch each other's mistakes before showing you. Keep a running file of mistakes and fixes. (10K) This is what the vault's `council-method` and `quality-score` already do.
- Apply mental models (second-order thinking, inversion, first principles) when deciding.
- Had Claude turn his TikTok analytics into a NotebookLM podcast instead of a text report.
- On models (Nov 2026): Fable 5.2 is the strongest but burns usage fast; he stays on Opus 5.5 day to day and uses Fable for max-intelligence work or reviews.
